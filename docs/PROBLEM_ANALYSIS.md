# OpenConvert — Problem Analysis: Why PDF→EPUB Is Hard

**Status:** Final for v1 planning. Companion to `DECISIONS.md` (source of truth for what OpenConvert builds) and `IR_SKETCH.md` (terminology: `oc-model` types, stage names).
**Date:** 2026-09-09
**Scope:** what actually goes wrong converting PDF to reflowable EPUB, why, how OpenConvert's pipeline addresses each failure class, and where the original brief's framing needed correction.
**Convention:** citations are `(Rn §x.y)`, with a URL attached to load-bearing statistics on first use. ## 1. Thesis

PDF→EPUB is not a format conversion. It is a **document-structure inference problem wearing a format-conversion costume**. A PDF content stream is a sequence of drawing operators — glyphs positioned by coordinate, images placed by matrix, paths stroked and filled. Nothing in the format requires it to contain a word, a line, a paragraph, a heading, a footnote, or a reading order; those are properties of the *document*, not of the *file*. An EPUB, by contrast, is a semantic tree: XHTML elements with roles (`<h2>`, `<aside epub:type="footnote">`, `<figure>`), wrapped in a package that declares a spine and a navigation document. Converting from one to the other means **reconstructing a tree that was never serialized**, from geometric and typographic evidence alone.

Empirically, almost none of that tree survives in the file as data. Bast & Korzen's JCDL 2017 benchmark — still the most rigorous cross-tool evaluation available — put it plainly: *"PDFs do not represent their text as lines or words, but rather as individual characters to draw at specific locations on a page"* (R1 §A.0, https://ad-publications.cs.uni-freiburg.de/benchmark.pdf). The one channel that could carry structure directly — the optional Tagged-PDF `/StructTreeRoot` — is present in **12.6% of 19,997 sampled scholarly PDFs from 2014–2023, and the rate has been declining since 2019** (Kumar et al., ASSETS 2024, R1 §A.10, https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03022v1). Of the sliver that is tagged, only **3.2% met all six accessibility criteria measured (appropriate nesting, table headers, tab order, alt text, default language, tagging itself); 74.9% met none**; and of the 8.5% carrying *some* alt text, **95% of it was non-descriptive boilerplate ("Image", "Figure", "Logo")** — leaving roughly **0.03% of the corpus with meaningful alt text** (R1 §A.10, same source). Trade books and older scans are, if anything, worse-tagged than the scholarly-PDF sample this study measured.

The consequence for architecture is direct: **every stage of the pipeline is inference under uncertainty**, and the decisive engineering question is not "which extractor is best" but **"how do we detect and repair our own inference errors."** That is precisely the axis on which every existing open-source tool — Calibre included, and the 2025–26 VLM-based document tools especially — is weakest (§6). It is also the axis `DECISIONS.md` D13.4–D13.7 (the conservation law, the escalation gates, the validate→repair loop) is built around, and this document exists to justify that emphasis with evidence rather than assert it.

A second, quieter thesis follows from the first: because structure must be *reconstructed*, every deterministic heuristic has a **measured, non-trivial error rate**, not a binary correct/incorrect behavior. Bast & Korzen's own best-in-class tool (a bespoke rule engine, not a naive extractor) still missed 13% of expected paragraph breaks and fabricated spurious ones on 4.2% of lines (R1 §A.0). Dehyphenation with a dictionary alone gets keep-hyphen recall of ~32%; a kilobyte-scale classifier lifts that to ~86% (R2 §B.7, Hernaes 2019, https://ad-publications.cs.uni-freiburg.de/theses/Bachelor_Mari_Hernaes_2019.pdf). On a 300-page novel with roughly 2,000 hyphenated line breaks, an 86%-accurate dehyphenator still produces on the order of 280 wrong words. **"Deterministic first" therefore cannot mean "deterministic and therefore correct."** It has to mean *deterministic, confidence-scored, and backstopped by validation and repair* — the framing `DECISIONS.md` §D13.4–D13.7 adopts, and the one this document's evidence base supports throughout.

## 2. Root causes in the PDF imaging model

Nearly the whole failure taxonomy in §3–§4 traces back to a small number of structural facts about the format. This table is the "why" behind almost every row of §4.

| # | Fact about the PDF imaging model | Downstream consequence | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Text is `Tj`/`TJ` operators positioning **glyph codes** (font-local integers), not Unicode. The `ToUnicode` CMap that recovers Unicode is *optional*. | Encoding failures: mojibake, placeholder glyphs, silent character loss on subsetted fonts. | R1 §A.1.1 |
| 2 | There is no word, line, or paragraph object. Inter-glyph spacing is a kerning number inside a `TJ` array or a `Td`/`TD`/`Tm` matrix move; a "space" is often not a character at all. | Word-splitting/merging errors; paragraph-boundary reconstruction is "even more challenging than word identification and word order" (Bast & Korzen). | R1 §A.1.6, §A.2.2 |
| 3 | Drawing order in the content stream is the **producer's convenience order** (often column-by-column or object-by-object), not reading order. | Multi-column interleaving; footnote/body mixing; sidebar and caption text injected mid-sentence. | R1 §A.2.4 |
| 4 | Semantics live only in the optional structure tree (Tagged PDF), present in 12.6% of files and declining. | Headings, lists, tables, footnotes, reading order — all must be inferred from geometry, essentially always. | R1 §A.10 |
| 5 | The page is a physical, fixed-size artifact; text, images, and vectors are positioned in one absolute coordinate space per page, and that space is not even consistent (`MediaBox`, `CropBox`, `/Rotate`, `/UserUnit` can all differ). | Carrying PDF geometry into EPUB CSS produces a non-reflowable, fixed-layout document — "the single biggest architectural trap" (R1 §A.5) — and mixing coordinate spaces silently drops content (a real, shipping-2026 bug: Marker's `MarginaliaProcessor` deleted body text on non-zero-origin CropBoxes, issue #1072, R1 §A.3.1). | R1 §A.5, §A.3.1 |
| 6 | Fonts may be non-embedded (substituted at render time, so size histograms shift across producer runs), Type 3 (glyphs are arbitrary drawn content, not characters), or use presentation-form/ligature glyphs with no canonical decomposition. | Font-size heading-clustering noise; silent character dropout in Type 3 runs; ligature corruption (`"fire"` → `" re"`). | R1 §A.1.2, §A.1.3, §A.4.6 |
| 7 | A "PDF" is not one artifact. It is at least four: clean born-digital text, born-digital text with broken encoding, a scan with an invisible OCR text layer, and a pure image scan — and real books mix these within one file (plates, facsimile pages, appendices). | A converter that does not classify per page will apply the wrong pipeline to a majority of pages in a mixed document. | R1 §A.7.1, §D.3 |
| 8 | Images and vector graphics are independent, disconnected objects: stored size ≠ rendered size (CTM scaling), transparency is a *separate* SMask object, scans routinely arrive as JBIG2/JPX/MRC composites, and vector paths carry no semantic tag (a table rule and a decorative flourish are the same primitive). | Bloated or blurred images; black boxes where alpha should be; broken/tripled images from undecoded scan codecs; vanished charts and diagrams (vectors are simply not text and are usually dropped). | R1 §A.6 |

**Design implication.** Every one of these eight facts is a property of the *format*, not a bug in any particular file — so no amount of "better parsing" alone eliminates them. They can only be handled by (a) normalizing geometry into one space at ingest, (b) classifying documents and pages before choosing a pipeline, (c) treating structure as *inferred with confidence*, never as *read*, and (d) validating the output against the input rather than trusting the inference. `DECISIONS.md` D13.3 (one normalized geometry space, asserted invariant), D13.10 (per-page classification as a first-class verdict), and D13.4/D13.7 (the conservation law and repair loop) are direct responses to facts 5, 7, and 1–4/6/8 respectively.

## 5. Language-specific problems

### 5.1 German

Three compounding hazards, all with direct evidence, none solved by a naive dehyphenator:

1. **Ambiguous compound joins.** `nachtritt` is either `nach-tritt` or `Nacht-ritt`. The TeX German hyphenation-pattern documentation explicitly refuses to break such words except at positions valid under *both* readings (R1 §A.2.3, https://ctan.math.washington.edu/tex-archive/languages/hyphenation/dehyph-exptl/dehyph-exptl.pdf).
2. **Pre-1996 orthography `ck`→`k-k`.** `drucken` was split as `druk-ken` under the old spelling reform. The CTAN documentation states plainly that these *"Spezialtrennungen … kann TEX nicht automatisch behandeln"* — TeX cannot handle these special hyphenations automatically. **A dehyphenator that naively joins `druk` + `ken` produces `drukken`, a non-word.** Any pre-1996 German scan will hit this on every `ck` break; no PDF→EPUB tool surveyed handles it.
3. **Triple-consonant restoration.** Pre-reform `Schiffahrt` vs. post-reform `Schifffahrt` — joining `Schiff-` + `fahrt` may require *inserting* a letter depending on orthography epoch, and the same source document warns the three-consonant rule *"birgt die Gefahr irreführender Trennungen"* (carries a risk of misleading hyphenations).

The document's own conclusion generalizes past dehyphenation: *"Prinzipiell können mit einem solchen Algorithmus nicht alle möglichen Wörter korrekt getrennt werden"* — in principle no such algorithm can hyphenate all German words correctly (R1 §A.2.3). Beyond hyphenation, **Fugenlaute** (the linking `-s-`/`-n-`/`-es-` in compounds like `Bundesgesetz`) and the **ß/ss alternation** (Swiss orthography uses `ss` exclusively; pre-1996 rules differ from post-1996) both affect dictionary-lookup-based dehyphenation and any compound-splitter's accuracy — a joined form's absence from a word list can mean "wrong join" or simply "word list lacks this legitimate Fugenlaut variant," and the two must not be conflated.

**What OpenConvert does:** the deterministic dehyphenation cascade (`oc-text`, D13.6, D15) is: strip soft hyphens → line-final-hyphen + lowercase-next-line candidate → **in-document dictionary** (Calibre's technique of treating the book's own vocabulary as ground truth, which is self-calibrating to proper nouns and neologisms) → language lexicon. For German specifically, both halves being independently attested capitalized nouns is treated as evidence to *keep* the hyphen (`Nord-Süd-Achse`, `Goethe-Institut` are genuinely hyphenated, not line-broken). Word-frequency lists for German are **core data generated by OpenConvert's own tooling from CC0/PD text (DTA plain text)**, not a bundled GPL hunspell dictionary (D15) — this closes the licensing gap the red team flagged (RT B10) but leaves German dehyphenation quality dependent on that data asset actually being built and validated before v1 ships.

### 5.2 Turkish

Two independent, compounding traps, and a genuine evidence gap:

1. **Dotted/dotless I case folding.** Turkish has four I-letters: `İ`/`i` (dotted) and `I`/`ı` (dotless), and they do **not** map the way ASCII case-folding assumes. `"QUIT".toLowerCase()` under Turkish rules does not equal `"quit"` (R1 §A.2.3, https://www.i18nguy.com/unicode/turkish-i18n.html; https://haacked.com/archive/2012/07/05/turkish-i-problem-and-why-you-should-care.aspx/). **Any dehyphenation, heading-keyword match, small-caps detection, or dictionary lookup that case-folds using an invariant/English locale will silently corrupt Turkish text** — lowercasing a heading to test `^chapter` will mangle `İ` into `i̇` (a two-codepoint sequence: `i` + combining dot above, U+0307), changing the string's length and breaking any position-based logic downstream. Worse, NFC does **not** recompose that two-codepoint sequence back to a single `i` — so case-fold-then-normalize is lossy in a way that survives all the way into the shipped EPUB.
2. **Agglutination weakens the dictionary signal.** Turkish hyphenates at syllable boundaries, and because the language is agglutinative, the joined form of a broken word is very often a *productively inflected* surface form absent from any finite word list (`kitaplarımızdan` — "from our books" — is a legal word built from a stem plus four suffixes, and no static dictionary will contain it). This makes the same dictionary-hit-rate signal that works reasonably for German and English **structurally weaker for Turkish**; a morphological analyzer (Zemberek/TRmorph-class, used as an *acceptor* — "is this a valid surface form?" — rather than a word-list lookup) is the right deterministic tool, though R1 found no published evaluation of this approach specifically for post-OCR or line-break dehyphenation.

**Honest evidence gap, stated plainly:** neither R1 nor R10 found a single published study of Turkish dehyphenation, Turkish OCR post-correction, or Turkish layout analysis (R1 §A.2.3, R10 §9.1, both marked `[UNVERIFIED]`). The reasoning above is sound from language structure but is not empirically anchored the way the German and English cases are. `DECISIONS.md` D4/D13.6 respond to this correctly by (a) never enabling LLM-based OCR post-correction for any language by default, and (b) treating Turkish dehyphenation residuals with the same fail-closed default (keep the hyphen) as every other language, rather than assuming confidence the evidence does not support.

**What OpenConvert does:** case-folding used **only** to build lookup keys, never applied to emitted text (D13.4 — "Text is never case-folded"); Turkish-aware folding rules specifically named in D13.11's language handling. Dehyphenation falls back to keep-hyphen (fail-closed) whenever neither the joined nor split form is independently attested — which, per the agglutination point above, will fire more often for Turkish than for German or English, and that asymmetry should be visible in the eventual per-language false-repair-rate metrics (D17).

### 5.3 English

The lowest-risk of the three target languages, but not risk-free: English still has the general dehyphenation ambiguity class (`self-esteem` vs. `selfesteem`, `re-enter` vs. `reenter`), Calibre's own English-only heading regexes (`Chapter N`, Roman numerals, `Part X`) are a useful deterministic floor but miss any book using titles instead of numbered chapters, and the ligature/space-synthesis issues in §3.1 apply identically. English is the language every deterministic heuristic in this document was implicitly validated against first (Bast & Korzen, GROBID, DocLayNet, Gopher statistics are all English-centric benchmarks); the DE/TR evidence gaps above are therefore *relative* to a comparatively well-evidenced English baseline, not an absolute absence of risk.

### 5.4 RTL and CJK (lower priority per brief, explicitly out of v1)

**RTL (Arabic/Hebrew).** Two orthogonal problems: (a) many producers emit text runs in *visual* rather than *logical* order, requiring reversal before the extracted string is meaningful; (b) Arabic presentation forms (U+FB50–U+FEFF), used by the rendering font, must be mapped back to base letters and re-shaped by the reading system. Calibre: "complex text, like right to left languages … will not convert correctly" (R1 §A.9). PDF.js at least reports per-item direction (`dir: 'ttb'|'ltr'|'rtl'`).

**CJK.** Vertical writing (`Identity-V`), no inter-word spaces (meaning every space-synthesis heuristic in §3.1 must be *disabled*, not just tuned, or it will insert spaces inside words), and CID-keyed fonts where `ToUnicode` is more often present but `/Registry`+`/Ordering` fallback CMaps may still be needed. The general rule this implies: **all whitespace-based heuristics must be script-conditional**, never a single global threshold.

Both are explicitly out of v1 (D16). They are noted here, per the brief, because the architectural lesson generalizes even to in-scope languages: any heuristic tuned on Latin-script assumptions (word spacing, case folding, hyphenation) needs an explicit language/script gate, not silent global application — the same principle §5.2's Turkish case-folding trap illustrates for a language that *is* in scope.

### 6.3 The "silent failure" pattern in modern tools

The strongest strategic claim available from this research is that **no surveyed tool — old or 2026-vintage, heuristic or VLM-based — checks its own output against its own input.** Five concrete, dated failures from actively-maintained 2025–26 tools:

- **Marker #1091**: silent image loss on ~14% of pages in a mixed text/code/screenshot PDF — "no error or log output" (R1 §A.6).
- **Marker #1092**: a single source figure exported as multiple separate image files, from the strip-tiling defect class (§3.3).
- **Marker #1081**: a deterministic 58-word fabrication on a bleed-through page (the model wove fluent, entirely invented text from a faint mirrored passage) **and, separately, a silently dropped 39-word paragraph from a clean control page** — fabrication and deletion, in the same tool, on the same benchmark run.
- **Marker #1072**: `MarginaliaProcessor` deletes body text on PDFs with a non-zero CropBox origin — the coordinate-space-mixing bug this document's §2 row 5 and §3.2 CropBox row describe directly, confirmed shipping.
- **Docling**: silently drops text located inside figures (R1 §C.3).
- **MinerU**: open issues for missing block text (R1 §C.3, issues #5305/#5304/#5293).

**Four of five Marker issues alone are silent deletions or fabrications with no error output** (R1 §B.1). This is not a criticism of any one tool's engineering quality — Marker's own architecture (§6.1) is, by its authors' own description, a well-designed confidence cascade. It is evidence that **validating output against input is an unsolved problem industry-wide**, which is exactly why `DECISIONS.md` D13.4 (the conservation law) and D13.7 (the validate→repair loop) are load-bearing architectural decisions rather than optional polish — every failure in this list would have been caught by a character-conservation check, an image-count parity check, or both.

## 7. Where OpenConvert genuinely does better

Ten concrete differentiators, each backed by an identified gap in every surveyed alternative:

1. **The conservation-law checker** (D13.4). Text-mass conservation with a closed reason taxonomy and per-reason budgets, image-count parity, and end-to-end release gating (`I-7`) are deterministic, cheap, and — per §6.3 — would have caught every dated silent-failure bug found in this research. No surveyed tool does this.
2. **A document tree IR, not Markdown** (D13.3). Every 2025–26 VLM tool converges on Markdown/JSON as its intermediate/output format, which structurally caps expressiveness below what EPUB needs (§6.1). `oc-model`'s typed `Section`/`Content`/`Span` tree can represent footnotes, verse, small caps, and `epub:type` semantics that Markdown cannot.
3. **Per-page classification as a first-class, user-overridable verdict** (D13.10). `text`/`broken-text`/`ocr-sandwich`/`image-only`/`mixed`/`blank` is exposed, not implicit — directly closing the gap R1 §D.3 identifies (a converter treating "PDF→EPUB" as one problem when it is at least four).
4. **Outline↔heading agreement as a free semi-supervised signal** (R1 §C.4.2). The PDF outline, when present, supplies N supervised heading positions at zero cost; using it to calibrate the font-size threshold for the rest of the book — rather than either trusting it blindly or ignoring it — turns an unsupervised problem into a semi-supervised one, per document. HiPS's finding that TOC-based matching alone reaches median P_ED ≥ 0.9, beating every font-clustering and ML approach measured (R2 §B.5), is the direct evidence this is worth doing first.
5. **Per-document adaptation** (R1 §C.4.3). A book is internally consistent — its own body font, leading, indent, header text, footnote font, and drop-cap pattern are a coherent signal that generalizes far better within one book than any cross-book heuristic. Calibre does a weak version of this (a 20-page header scan); nobody does the full version.
6. **Encoding decipherment** (post-v1, D16). When a broken `ToUnicode` mapping is a monoalphabetic substitution — which the `7KH`/`THE` failure case in §3.1 proves it often is — n-gram frequency analysis against a language model solves it deterministically. R1 found no open-source PDF→EPUB tool doing this; it is a strong, cheap, high-value differentiator explicitly deferred rather than dropped.
7. **Vector→SVG instead of vector-dropped** (post-v1, D16). SVG is legal in EPUB 3 content documents, scales correctly, and reflows. No surveyed OSS converter emits it; v1 rasterizes as an interim step specifically so this upgrade path stays open.
8. **Accessibility metadata for free.** EPUB Accessibility 1.1 mandates `schema:accessMode`/`accessibilityFeature`/`accessibilityHazard`; a converter that already knows whether it OCR'd, whether images exist, and whether alt text was generated can emit honest values at near-zero marginal cost (R1 §C.4.6, R5 §A12) — and, per §6.3's evidence that even the 8.5% of PDFs claiming alt text are 95% boilerplate, doing this honestly is itself a differentiator.
9. **`page-list` navigation from source page numbers** (R1 §C.4.7). A reflowed EPUB can still carry citable print pagination, and the converter is uniquely positioned to emit it because it *knows* where the page breaks were. Calibre does not attempt this.
10. **Honest confidence, not silent best-effort.** Every decision in `oc-model` carries a `Confidence{method, signals, escalated, fallback_used}` record; warnings are structured, localized, and reference specific block ids (D13.3, D13.11). Combined with items 1–3 above, this means a user-facing quality report can name *specific, located* uncertainties — "3 tables were rendered as images because their structure could not be recovered" — rather than a tool that either silently degrades (§6.3) or gives no signal at all.

## 8. Problems the original brief missed or assumed wrongly

Nine items, drawn from R1 Part D and the round-2 red team, presented here because they materially changed the shape of `DECISIONS.md` and are easy to re-litigate accidentally if this reasoning is not visible.

1. **Coordinate-space normalization is not exotic — it is a shipping-2026 bug class.** Mixing `CropBox`, `MediaBox`, and rendered-image space silently deleted body text in Marker (#1072, §3.2, §6.3). It belongs in the taxonomy as its own row (§4 row 28), not folded into "geometry stuff," and `DECISIONS.md` D13.3 makes normalization an *asserted invariant* specifically because of this precedent.
2. **Fake bold via double-drawn glyphs is a distinct defect from OCR-sandwich duplication**, even though both manifest as "duplicated text." The fix differs: bbox-overlap dedup for fake bold, a render-mode check for OCR sandwiches. Conflating them (as the original brief's phrasing risked doing) would have produced a single detector that handles neither case well; `DECISIONS.md` D13.4 gives them separate `Reason` variants (`OverdrawDedup` vs. `OcrLayerDuplicate`) with separate budgets for exactly this reason.
3. **Language detection needs to be a mandatory pipeline stage, not an item under "metadata."** EPUB requires `dc:language`; hyphenation, TTS, and locale-correct case folding (§5.2's Turkish trap) all depend on it; and PDF metadata is unreliable for this field specifically (§3.4). `DECISIONS.md` D13.11 makes language detection from body text unconditional, never metadata-trusted.
4. **Producer fingerprinting is cheap, deterministic, and nobody does it.** `/Producer`/`/Creator` name the toolchain (pdfTeX, InDesign, Word, Ghostscript, ABBYY, Quark), and each has characteristic failure signatures — pdfTeX ships Type 1/3 ligature problems, Word emits per-run text fragments, scanners emit MRC triples. `DECISIONS.md` D13.10 records this in the report and uses a short, explicit parameter table per producer family; the red team (RT A9) additionally makes producer stratification a corpus-honesty requirement, not just a runtime nicety.
5. **Poetry/verse has no deterministic answer anywhere in the surveyed literature — it deserves to be named as such, not implied.** It is the one row in the 48-item taxonomy marked ○○○, and it is disproportionately important for the literary long tail a local-first ebook tool serves (§4 row 19). It is also, not coincidentally, the strongest evidenced case *for* a bounded per-block LLM call (R10 §6.13, D13.6) — a text LLM's linguistic judgment (metre, rhyme, line-break placement) succeeds exactly where geometry cannot distinguish verse from a narrow indented quotation.
6. **Wide tables are a UX problem, not only an extraction-accuracy problem.** Perfect table-structure recovery (TEDS near 1.0) still produces an unreadable table on a 6-inch e-reader screen if emitted as a bare `<table>`. The pragmatic answer — `overflow-x:auto` wrapping, or an image fallback with extracted text in `<details>` — is a *presentation* decision independent of extraction quality, and the brief's framing (which measured tables purely as an extraction-accuracy problem) missed this entirely (R1 §A.4.3).
7. **The AGPL boundary must be decided before architecture, not after.** MuPDF/PyMuPDF is the single most capable text-extraction backend surveyed and it is AGPLv3; Poppler is GPL; several VLM tools' weights carry OpenRAIL-M revenue thresholds. For a distributed desktop app this is a load-bearing decision, not a footnote — it is the reason `DECISIONS.md` D3 chose PDFium (BSD-3) over the technically stronger MuPDF, and why D15 excludes MuPDF/PyMuPDF/Poppler/MinerU/epubveri/Ultralytics-derived weights/Surya/Marker weights explicitly by name.
8. **"Deterministic first" must mean "confidence-scored, with a repair loop," not "deterministic and therefore trustworthy."** §1 already makes this argument from the dehyphenation and paragraph-boundary error rates; it is restated here because it is the single most consequential correction to the brief's likely reading, and it is why `DECISIONS.md` D17 exists as a named policy (thresholds carry a `source` provenance field) rather than an implicit assumption.
9. **The LLM's job is adjudication of deterministic ambiguity, not transcription of hard pages.** The brief's natural reading — deterministic pipeline, LLM as a fallback transcriber for hard cases — is the evidence-contradicted framing. Every fabrication and degeneration failure catalogued in this document (Marker #1081's 58-word invention, Nougat's `[MISSING_PAGE]` false positives, dots.ocr's acknowledged repetitive-output failures) came from generative transcription. The high-value, low-risk LLM jobs are short-input/short-output classification and adjudication tasks — "is this trailing hyphen lexical," "is this block a footnote or body text," "which of two backends' readings is correct" — all under 200 tokens, all checkable, none capable of inventing a paragraph (R1 §D.1, R10 §4.1–4.3). `DECISIONS.md` D13.5–D13.6's four-gate escalation design and the once-per-book call shape are the direct architectural response.

A tenth item belongs here for completeness even though it is more a structural correction than a missed problem: **a PDF is at least four distinct artefacts** (clean born-digital text, born-digital text with broken encoding, scan-with-OCR-layer, pure scan), and treating "PDF→EPUB" as one problem rather than a dispatch over four is the single deepest assumption error a naive reading of the brief could make (R1 §D.3, §3.4 above). `DECISIONS.md` D13.10's per-page classification is the fix.

## 9. Definition of "high-quality reflowable EPUB"

Nothing in the source landscape defines EPUB quality directly. OmniDocBench and olmOCR-bench — the two most-cited document-conversion benchmarks — measure *edit distance to a text/Markdown ground truth*, which is blind to every EPUB-specific property: semantic markup correctness, footnote linkage, TOC-to-heading binding, actual reflow behavior, and accessibility metadata (R1 §D.5; R9 §A.15–A.16). **OpenConvert has to define its own metric, or the project cannot converge on what "good" means** — this is R1's explicit warning and it is treated here as a requirement, not a suggestion.

The composite metric set (elaborated with literature-anchored thresholds and CI-tier placement in `TEST_STRATEGY.md`) has seven components:

1. **Character/word retention accuracy vs. the conservation ledger** — the `C_0`/`C(EPUB)` accounting from D13.4 directly, not a separate edit-distance computation. Target: zero unexplained loss (every removed character has a declared `Reason` within its stage's budget); this is a release gate (I-7), not a trend metric.
2. **Paragraph-boundary F1** — the Bast-Korzen NL±/P± framework (§1) is directly reusable: newline-missed rate, newline-spurious rate, tracked per producer stratum (R1 §D.5).
3. **Heading-tree agreement** — outline↔detected-heading agreement where an outline exists; internal well-formedness (no level skips, monotone with page order) everywhere; tracked as R9 §A.16's self-defined "TOC-F1" since no external baseline exists for book-length works.
4. **Footnote/caption/list role F1** — precision/recall on (marker, note-id) bijections and (caption, figure) associations; self-calibrated against hand-labeled fixtures, per R9 §A.16.
5. **Image conservation** — extraction count vs. emitted count parity, the direct countermeasure to the Marker #1091/#1092 failure class (§6.3).
6. **Conformance gates** — EPUBCheck error count = 0 and Ace-by-DAISY serious-violation count = 0 plus required accessibility-metadata presence, both binary and both CI-blocking (R5 §B7, R9 §A.15). These are the only two metrics in this list that are not judgment calls.
7. **A reflow smoke test at three viewport widths** — `scrollWidth > clientWidth` DOM overflow detection (R5 §D9), the deliberately engine-agnostic replacement for pixel comparison, which R5 establishes is close to meaningless between a fixed-geometry PDF page and a reflowed EPUB chapter.

Consistent with R9 §A.16's own recommendation, OpenConvert does **not** chase a single scalar "quality score." The corpus-honesty requirements in `DECISIONS.md` D18 (real strata vs. `ours(*)` synthetic strata, reported separately, a release cannot pass on synthetic strata alone) apply to every metric above — a metric computed only against WeasyPrint/Typst-rendered fixtures measures the pipeline's fit to clean, tagged, single-producer input, which is precisely the population §1 shows is unrepresentative of real books.

## 10. Notes for the Chief Architect

This document was written to support, not second-guess, `DECISIONS.md` — and on every point where the two documents could plausibly seem to diverge, the divergence resolves to "this document explains evidence `DECISIONS.md` already incorporated," not a genuine disagreement. Two things are worth surfacing explicitly rather than leaving implicit:

- **The German and Turkish dehyphenation quality gap is a data-asset dependency, not yet a shipped capability.** §5.1–§5.2 make clear that OpenConvert's deterministic dehyphenation quality for German depends on the CC0-sourced frequency-list data D15 commits to generating, and for Turkish depends on reasoning-from-language-structure that has no published empirical anchor at all. Neither is a flaw in the architecture — D17's fail-closed default (keep the hyphen when uncertain) is the correct response to genuine uncertainty — but the *first* real quality signal for either language will come from the calibration corpus D17/RT-C4 describes building, not from a number in this document. Treat every German/Turkish dehyphenation claim above as a design target, not a measured result.
- **Poetry/verse (taxonomy row 19) is flagged ○○○ deliberately and should stay that way through v1.** There is no dataset, no heuristic, and no published benchmark for this specific classification anywhere in the research base (R1 §D.6 item 5, R10 §6.13). It is simultaneously the strongest evidenced case for a bounded per-block LLM call in the entire report (text-only judgment, bounded input, CSS-class-only output, structurally incapable of corrupting text) — meaning it is a good candidate for **early** post-`ai.enabled=true` validation precisely because a wrong answer there is cheap to detect and cheap to fix, which makes it a low-risk place to gain confidence in the escalation-gate machinery before trusting it on higher-stakes decisions like heading-role assignment.

No other item in this document's problem map, taxonomy, or tooling survey contradicts a stated decision in `DECISIONS.md`; where the red-team review (`RED_TEAM_REVIEW.md`) identified a genuine defect in an earlier draft of the architecture (the conservation-invariant scope, the Qwen3.5-2B default, the `--cache-reuse` claim, and the others in that review's Part A), `DECISIONS.md` v2 already reflects the corrected position, and this document cites the corrected version throughout rather than the superseded one.
