# R7 — Test Corpus and Synthetic PDF Generation
**OpenConvert research track R7.** Research only; nothing implemented. Compiled 2026-09-09.

**Methodology note on this session's tool limits:** This research combined live web search and page fetches; the WebSearch budget was exhausted partway through (200/200 calls), after which research continued via direct WebFetch of official pages plus the researcher's trained knowledge. Every claim below is tagged:
- **[VERIFIED via fetch]** — confirmed by directly fetching the cited URL during this session (quoted/paraphrased from that fetch).
- **[VERIFIED via search]** — confirmed from WebSearch result titles/snippets pointing to an authoritative URL, cited.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** — asserted from general/trained knowledge because a live check failed (404, robots block, rate limit) or wasn't attempted; **must be re-confirmed before any file is actually ingested into the corpus.**

**Hard rule carried through this whole report:** nothing here is "cleared." Every candidate file needs a final, individual license check (snapshot the license page/text, record the URL and date) at the moment it is actually added to the corpus, not just at research time. Licenses, hosting, and even entire sites can change (example found in this research: the CIA World Factbook was discontinued in Feb 2026 — see Part A).

## Executive Summary

- **Best real-world sources for redistributable, high-quality PDFs**: Standard Ebooks (CC0, but ships EPUB/AZW3 only — PDF must be generated by us, which is actually a *feature*, see Part B), Project Gutenberg (plain-text-derived works are effectively free once the boilerplate license/header is stripped, but PG does not ship native PDFs and imposes verbatim-copy + trademark conditions on redistributing *their* files), Internet Archive public-domain scans (PDF + hOCR + ABBYY OCR bundled, `NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT` rights field, confirmed), arXiv papers individually marked CC BY 4.0 (confirmed live on a real paper), PLOS and eLife (blanket CC BY 4.0 for all articles, confirmed), DocLayNet (CDLA-Permissive-1.0, confirmed, 80,863 pages / ~35.5GB, six document domains), and US federal government works (public domain under 17 U.S.C. §105, confirmed) — NASA history volumes, GAO reports, IRS forms, CRS reports, NIST SPs, court opinions.
- **Sources to explicitly avoid or flag**: RVL-CDIP/FUNSD (murky IIT-CDIP tobacco-litigation provenance, "research use" reputation, license not clean), DocBank (README literally says "DO NOT re-distribute our data" despite an Apache-2.0 badge on the mirror — a direct contradiction), PubLayNet (annotations are CDLA-Permissive but the *images* are separately PMC-licensed per article), Projekt Gutenberg-DE (claims copyright, browse-only, not redistributable), CommonCrawl/SAFEDOCS PDF corpus (real scraped web content of unclear per-file copyright — local eval only, never redistribute), UN documents (not automatically public domain).
- **Ground truth strategy**: the highest-leverage, lowest-cost source is generating PDFs *ourselves* from Standard Ebooks' CC0 XHTML via WeasyPrint/Typst/Chromium — because we own the DOM, we know the exact heading tree, paragraph boundaries, footnote↔marker mapping, and image/caption placement, so scoring becomes exact-match rather than fuzzy. arXiv LaTeX source is the second-best (structure + real math). DocLayNet gives real per-page layout ground truth across six non-literary domains. PDF/UA tagged-PDF structure trees (Typst 0.14+ output, some accessibility-mandated real docs) are a third, underused source. A small hand-annotated golden set covers the "broken" category, which has no other ground truth. Recommended scoring: normalized CER/WER for text, heading-tree edit distance / F1 for structure, an order-based edit-distance metric for reading order (as used by OmniDocBench), TEDS for tables, LaTeX edit-distance for formulas.
- **Synthetic toolchain recommendation**: a two-stage pipeline — generate *valid, complex* PDFs with a high-level engine (Typst, Apache-2.0, confirmed tagged-PDF/PDF-UA-1 output as of 0.14, embeddable as a Rust crate; or WeasyPrint/Chromium for HTML-sourced ground-truth pairs), then *corrupt them on purpose* with low-level mutators (ReportLab's canvas for arbitrary content-stream ordering, or lopdf/pdf-writer/qpdf in Rust for stripping ToUnicode maps, shuffling operators, and breaking xrefs). qpdf's QDF mode is particularly valuable because it makes defects diffable and reviewable in git. Keep LaTeX and mutool (AGPL) out of the CI hot path.

## Part B — Ground-Truth Strategy

### B.1 Generate PDFs from known-structure sources (highest leverage)

**Standard Ebooks XHTML → our own PDF.** Because SE's markup is CC0 and semantically rich (proper `<h1>`–`<h6>`, `epub:type` attributes distinguishing chapters/poems/drama/front-matter, `<a epub:type="noteref">`/endnote structures, `<figure>`/`<figcaption>` for images), we can:
1. Parse the XHTML DOM directly into a ground-truth JSON schema: `{headings: [{level, text, id}], paragraphs: [...], footnotes: [{marker_id, body_text, referring_paragraph_id}], images: [{src, alt, caption, position}]}`.
2. Render that same XHTML to PDF with WeasyPrint (BSD, direct HTML/CSS→PDF) and, separately, with headless Chromium via Playwright, and — as a third independent rendering path — restructure the content into Typst markup and render via Typst (Apache-2.0). Three independent renderers from one ground-truth source lets us distinguish "our EPUB converter is wrong" from "this specific PDF renderer's quirk is wrong," which a single-renderer ground truth cannot do.
3. Score OpenConvert's PDF→EPUB output against the *original XHTML-derived* ground truth (not against the intermediate PDF), so the test is really "did we recover the true document structure," independent of which renderer produced the input PDF.

This is essentially a free, scalable (thousands of SE titles), exact-match ground-truth factory — the single best investment in R7.

**arXiv LaTeX → structure.** `\section`/`\subsection` give exact heading hierarchy; `\footnote` gives footnotes; `\includegraphics`+`\caption` give figure/caption pairs; `\begin{table}` gives table structure; math environments give exact LaTeX-source ground truth for formulas (uniquely valuable — no OCR pipeline needs to *guess* the equation, we already have its ground-truth string). arXiv also runs an HTML-conversion pipeline (`ar5iv`/arXiv's newer native HTML) that can serve as a secondary structured-source cross-check. Caveat: LaTeX macro complexity (custom commands, conditionals) means a naive LaTeX parser will fail on some fraction of real papers — budget for a curated subset of "LaTeX-parses-cleanly" papers rather than assuming every CC-BY arXiv paper is usable out of the box.

### B.2 Real annotated datasets as ground truth

DocLayNet (and, with appropriate per-document-rights caution, PubLayNet/OmniDocBench) supply **per-page bounding-box + category ground truth** on real, non-literary documents. This is complementary to B.1: it covers financial reports, patents, manuals, and government tenders that no literature-derived ground truth touches, but it is *page-level layout* ground truth, not full-document reading-order-across-pages ground truth in the same sense as a structured XHTML source. Use it to validate the layout-segmentation stage of the pipeline (does OpenConvert correctly classify a block as heading/table/footnote/figure?), and use B.1 to validate end-to-end document reconstruction.

### B.3 Tagged PDF / PDF/UA structure trees as ground truth

A tagged, PDF/UA-conformant PDF embeds a `/StructTree` with `/S` (structure type: H1, P, Figure, Table, etc.), `/K` (kids, giving reading order), and marked-content (`/MCID`) linking structure nodes to actual content-stream runs. Where such a PDF exists, **the structure tree itself is machine-extractable ground truth** without needing an external source document at all. Typst 0.14+ ships PDF/UA-1-tagged output *by default* (confirmed live — see Part C), so any PDF we generate synthetically via current Typst is automatically also a "real tagged-PDF ground truth" sample, at zero extra authoring cost. This is a genuinely underused technique: **every synthetic Typst-generated test fixture doubles as a tagged-PDF-ground-truth fixture for free.** Real-world tagged PDFs (some accessibility-mandated government/EU documents, especially post-European-Accessibility-Act June 2025) are a second, real-world source of this kind of ground truth — but tagging quality in the wild is inconsistent, so each candidate needs a quick QA pass (open the struct tree, sanity-check it isn't a rubber-stamped/empty tag set) before being trusted.

### B.4 Manual annotation for a small golden set

The "broken" category (malformed PDFs, Isartor-style spec violations, adversarially-generated defect fixtures) has **no** external structural ground truth by definition — the whole point is that the file doesn't cleanly encode its own structure. Budget for a small (~20–50 file) hand-annotated golden set here: manually mark expected reading order, heading levels, and footnote linkage for the hardest fixtures. This is expensive per-file but the set stays small because most of the "broken" category's value comes from *synthetic* defect injection (Part C), where the ground truth is known automatically because *we* generated the pre-corruption version.

### B.5 Recommended scoring metrics

| Aspect | Metric | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Body text fidelity | Normalized character error rate (CER) / word error rate (WER), i.e., Levenshtein edit distance divided by reference length | Standard OCR/ASR-derived metric (e.g., via a library like `jiwer` or a direct implementation); normalize away pure-whitespace/reflow differences before scoring |
| Heading structure | Precision/recall/F1 on (heading text, level) pairs, plus a tree-edit-distance between predicted and ground-truth heading trees | Tree-edit distance penalizes "right text, wrong level" or "right level, wrong nesting" more informatively than flat F1 alone |
| Reading order | Edit-distance between predicted and ground-truth linear sequence of block IDs, normalized by sequence length | This mirrors the approach OmniDocBench documents for its own "reading order" annotation dimension **[VERIFIED via fetch]** — adopting a compatible metric lets us cross-reference published numbers on OmniDocBench-derived subsets even where we can't redistribute OmniDocBench's own files |
| Table structure | TEDS (Tree-Edit-distance-based Similarity, from the PubTabNet lineage) — both structure-only and structure+content variants | Industry-standard table metric; OmniDocBench and most modern document-parsing benchmarks report TEDS, so this keeps our numbers comparable to published baselines |
| Formulas | Normalized edit distance on LaTeX string (or a formula-specific metric such as CDM if we adopt one) | Requires LaTeX ground truth — available directly from arXiv source (B.1), synthetically from Typst/LaTeX math input, or hand-transcribed for the small golden set |
| Images/figures | Count precision/recall (right number of images kept) + caption-to-image association accuracy + (where DocLayNet-style bounding boxes exist) positional IoU | Simple count-based check catches the most common real bug (images silently dropped) cheaply |
| Footnotes | Precision/recall of marker→footnote-body correct linkage | OpenConvert-specific; derivable exactly from SE ground-truth pairs (B.1) where the noteref↔note relationship is explicit in the source markup |
| Aggregate | A weighted combination across the above, reported per-category (simple/difficult/broken/edge) rather than as one global number | Prevents a large simple-document pass rate from masking failure on the small but important broken/edge set — report categories separately, the way OmniDocBench reports per-document-type scores |

### C.2 Recommended minimal toolchain

**Two-stage pipeline, not a single tool:**

1. **Generate valid-but-complex fixtures** with a high-level engine:
   - **Typst** (Apache-2.0, embeddable as a Rust crate, tagged-PDF-by-default in 0.14+) for multi-column layouts, footnotes, headers/footers, math — and get PDF/UA structure-tree ground truth *for free* as a side effect (Part B.3).
   - **WeasyPrint** (BSD, Python) specifically for the Standard-Ebooks-XHTML-→-PDF ground-truth-pairing use case (Part B.1), since it shares the HTML/CSS family with the source.
   - Optionally **Chromium via Playwright** as a second, independent renderer of the same XHTML source, to distinguish renderer quirks from converter bugs.

2. **Corrupt fixtures on purpose** with low-level mutators:
   - **ReportLab** (Python, BSD) or **pdf-writer** (Rust) when authoring a defect *from scratch* with full manual content-stream ordering control (arbitrary text order, overlapping text, missing fonts, weird sizes, header text mixed into body).
   - **lopdf** (Rust, MIT) or **qpdf** (Apache-2.0, C++/CLI, QDF mode) when *mutating an already-valid* fixture from step 1 — stripping ToUnicode maps, shuffling operators, breaking xrefs, damaging structure trees. **qpdf's QDF mode is the standout recommendation here** for keeping defects reviewable as git diffs.

3. **Scanned-document simulation** (separate small pipeline): render clean PDF → rasterize pages (mutool/pdfium/pdftoppm at 200–300dpi, generated offline only per the AGPL caution above, or a Rust `image`-crate-based renderer if a same-language path matters) → inject noise/skew/blur/JPEG artifacts (Pillow/OpenCV, or a Rust `image`-crate equivalent) → wrap as image-only PDF via **img2pdf** (LGPL) — optionally run Tesseract afterward to add a deliberately-imperfect OCR text layer, simulating an Internet-Archive-style scanned book.

**Language fit**: the brief flags "ideally in the project's main language." Typst, pdf-writer, krilla, and lopdf together cover generation, low-level construction, and mutation entirely within Rust with no Python or browser dependency — a strong, CI-light, single-language toolchain if OpenConvert itself is Rust-based (which the emphasis on Rust-crate embedding in the brief suggests). Keep the Python tools (ReportLab, WeasyPrint, img2pdf) as a secondary, still-fully-open-source track specifically for the XHTML ground-truth-pairing and scanned-simulation use cases where their ecosystem maturity (WeasyPrint's CSS engine, Tesseract's Python bindings) currently outpaces Rust equivalents. **Keep LaTeX and mutool out of the CI hot path entirely** — LaTeX for its size/fragility, mutool for its AGPL license risk to the shipped product.

## Part D — Corpus Organization

### D.1 Metadata schema (per file)

```json
{
  "id": "stable-slug-or-uuid",
  "title": "string",
  "source": {
    "name": "Project Gutenberg | Standard Ebooks | Internet Archive | arXiv | ... | synthetic-generator",
    "url": "canonical source URL",
    "retrieved_date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
    "archive_snapshot_url": "Wayback Machine URL, recorded as a fallback in case the source disappears"
  },
  "license": {
    "name": "CC0-1.0 | CC-BY-4.0 | CC-BY-SA-4.0 | PD-US-Gov | PD-old-work | CDLA-Permissive-1.0 | Apache-2.0 | MIT | ...",
    "url": "license text URL",
    "verified_by": "person/agent",
    "verified_date": "YYYY-MM-DD"
  },
  "sha256": "hex digest of the exact file",
  "file_size_bytes": 0,
  "pages": 0,
  "category": "simple | difficult | broken | edge-case",
  "difficulty": 1,
  "expected_problems": ["two-column", "footnotes", "rotated-pages", "image-only", "..."],
  "expected_output_characteristics": {
    "headings": 0, "images": 0, "tables": 0, "footnotes": 0, "languages": ["en"]
  },
  "ground_truth_type": "generated-from-xhtml | latex-source | doclaynet-annotation | tagged-pdf-structtree | manual-annotation | none",
  "ground_truth_ref": "path or URL to the ground-truth file",
  "generator": "typst-<version> | weasyprint-<version> | reportlab-<version> | lopdf-mutation-<script> | n/a-real-world",
  "defect_injection": ["shuffled-content-stream", "stripped-tounicode", "..."]
}
```

A CI check should refuse any commit that adds a corpus file without a fully-populated `license` block, and should validate `license.name` against an explicit **allowlist** (CC0-1.0, CC-BY-4.0, CC-BY-SA-3.0/4.0, PD-US-Gov, PD-old-work, CDLA-Permissive-1.0, Apache-2.0, MIT, BSD-3-Clause) and reject an explicit **blocklist** of known-bad statuses (research-only, DocBank-style "do not redistribute," CommonCrawl-derived, RVL-CDIP/FUNSD-derived, "license unknown").

### D.2 Storage

- **Do not vendor large binaries into the main git history.** Recommended approach: commit only the small, text-diffable metadata manifest (the JSON schema above) plus a **download script** that fetches each real-world file from its canonical source (or a project-controlled mirror) and verifies the recorded `sha256` before use — this keeps the main repo small and fast to clone, which matters a lot for an open-source project with many CI runs and contributor clones.
- **For synthetic fixtures**, prefer *not* storing the generated PDF at all when generation is deterministic: store the generator script + fixed seed/config + the (tiny) expected ground truth, and regenerate the PDF at test time. If cross-platform/cross-version determinism can't be guaranteed (worth testing explicitly for Typst/ReportLab/lopdf output), fall back to storing one golden binary per fixture plus a CI "regenerate-and-diff" check that fails loudly if the toolchain silently produces different output.
- **Git LFS** is workable for moderate total size but carries real downsides for an OSS project specifically: GitHub's free-tier LFS bandwidth is metered and can be exhausted by CI traffic across many contributor forks/clones — treat LFS as a fallback, not the default.
- **GitHub Releases** as a lightweight, free, versioned CDN for a `corpus.tar.gz` of cleared real-world files: the download script pulls from the release asset first (fast, stable, versioned) and falls back to the original source URL if the release asset is unavailable, always checking `sha256` either way.
- **Never vendor** DocLayNet's full 35.5GB or anything from the multi-terabyte CommonCrawl/SAFEDOCS corpus — those stay strictly "fetch a small subset on demand, for local/nightly use, per documented instructions," never part of the redistributed default corpus.

### D.3 Size budget

- **Fast CI path** (every PR): a curated ~30–50 file subset, target **≤100MB total**, covering at least one item per category (simple/difficult/broken/edge) — small literature files, a handful of DocLayNet-subset pages, Isartor's small conformance files, and a handful of synthetic fixtures regenerated at test time (near-zero storage cost).
- **Nightly/full path**: a larger few-hundred-file, **~2–5GB target cap** corpus (larger literary works, more DocLayNet pages, more arXiv/PLOS/eLife papers, the large NASA volumes), downloaded on demand by the CI job, not vendored in git, checked against the sha256 manifest.
- **Never-vendored, reference-only tier**: full DocLayNet (35.5GB), CommonCrawl/SAFEDOCS (8TB) — documented as external tools with their own download instructions for contributors doing local, non-redistributed evaluation.

### D.4 Keeping the corpus license-clean over time

- Treat `Part A.0`'s per-source table as a living document, not a one-time check — re-verify each source's license at least annually, and immediately if a source's hosting changes (this research already surfaced one live example: the CIA World Factbook's reported February 2026 discontinuation).
- For any dataset with a two-layer license (DocLayNet/PubLayNet-style: annotation license ≠ underlying-document license), **record and satisfy both layers explicitly** in the metadata, not just the more permissive one.
- Maintain a clearly separate, non-redistributed **"local eval only"** area (e.g. a `LOCAL_EVAL_ONLY.md` plus a download script requiring an explicit opt-in flag) for anything like CommonCrawl/SAFEDOCS that is useful for local robustness testing but must never ship as part of the open-source redistributed test suite — making the redistribution boundary a deliberate, hard-to-bypass-by-accident mechanism rather than just a policy note.
- Default to exclusion on any ambiguity: DocBank, RVL-CDIP, FUNSD, Projekt Gutenberg-DE, and unverified DOAJ/DergiPark articles all stay out of the redistributed corpus until someone actively resolves the flagged uncertainty, rather than being included pending disproof.
